About / How to use

Model. Humans follow an SEIR model and Aedes mosquitoes an SEI model. A clean-up campaign cuts the recruitment of new adult mosquitoes by the chosen intensity from its start week until the end of the season; adults already alive die off at their natural rate. The equations are solved with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method (time step 0.5 days), the same code as the notebook ported to JavaScript.

Calibration. The default transmission rate, reporting fraction and starting infections are the posterior medians from a Metropolis MCMC fit to weekly reported dengue cases in Colombo District, epidemiological weeks 20–36 of 2026 (Epidemiology Unit, Weekly Epidemiological Report).

  • Move the sliders, then press Start to watch the season unfold week by week. Pause stops it; moving a slider updates the curves and outputs immediately. Reset restores the default settings.
  • Grey dashed line: no campaign. Blue line: with the campaign. Black dots: reported cases. The orange line marks the campaign start.
  • Try this: set intensity to 50% and the start to 8 weeks: the peak roughly halves. Now move the start to 2 weeks: almost no change, because removing breeding sites takes 3–4 weeks to show up in cases (mosquito lifespan, then about 10 days inside the mosquito and about 5.5 days in the person).
  • The checks at the bottom recompute two notebook reference values with the default settings; both must agree to within 1%.

Limitations. One deterministic curve (the uncertainty bands are in the notebook), homogeneous mixing across the district, one serotype, and a campaign that keeps working at a constant level. The flat pre-monsoon weeks before week 20 are not modelled.
